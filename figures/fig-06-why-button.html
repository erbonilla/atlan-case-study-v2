Depth · Threshold

Why threshold

Threshold intervals develop lactate clearance — the rate at which your body processes accumulating lactate during sustained efforts. Repeating this stimulus at the boundary between sustainable and unsustainable pace raises the work rate you can hold for an extended duration.

Mechanism
  • Increased mitochondrial density in slow-twitch fibers
  • Elevated capillarization around working muscle
  • Improved enzymatic activity for aerobic energy production

These adaptations distinguish trained endurance athletes from untrained populations. The standard prescription is 4–6 repetitions of 5–15 minutes at threshold pace, with short recoveries — the partial recovery is the point.

Reference

Maglischo, E. W. (2003). Swimming Fastest (2nd ed.). Champaign, IL: Human Kinetics, pp. 348–356.

1 Affordance 22 px target, Tide outline

The "i" sits inline with the term it explains — not floating in a separate column. Outlined, not filled. Quiet enough to ignore; visible enough to find.

2 Modal architecture Bottom sheet, not popup

A sheet emerges from below; the underlying session view stays partially visible. The user is still in their workout context, not interrupted by it.

3 Voice ratio 95 % Sage · 5 % Hero

The strongest Sage surface in the product. No motivational framing. No "great question!" warmth. Mechanism first, prescription second, attribution third.

4 Citation discipline Author · year · pages

Formal scholarly format. Page range. A real source — not a generic "Studies show…" gesture. Tappable in production to deep-link to Google Scholar or the publisher.

Citation methodology

Every "why" carries a source, or the "why" doesn't ship.

01

Author, year, page range. No vague references, no "research suggests" hedges, no decorative footnote glyphs. The reader can find the source in fewer than 60 seconds.

02

Textbook, peer-reviewed paper, or systematic review only. No blog posts, podcast clips, or coach-of-the-month opinions. Tertiary sources (Wikipedia) are excluded by default.

03

One citation per concept, not per claim. A modal explaining one biomechanical idea cites one canonical source for that idea. Citation density above one signals the explanation hasn't been edited enough.

04

The citation is visible, not hidden in a footer. Putting the source at the bottom of a long scroll suggests it's optional. The user should see attribution at the same scroll depth as the claim.

The Optimizer can read the science. They just don't have time to look it up at 9 PM. The "Why" button puts the source one tap from the term, every time.
