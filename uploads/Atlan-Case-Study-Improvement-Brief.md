# Atlan Case Study — Change and Improvement Brief

**Project:** Atlan Performance — Liquid Precision & The Quiet Sage  
**Case study:** <https://case-study-atlan.vercel.app/>  
**Repository:** <https://github.com/erbonilla/case-study-atlan>  
**Prepared:** July 24, 2026  
**Purpose:** Turn the existing long-form concept study into a concise, credible portfolio case study that demonstrates product judgment, environmental UX, accessibility, bilingual design, and honest evidence handling.

---

## 1. Executive diagnosis

Atlan already contains several unusually strong portfolio signals:

- A memorable strategic reframe: life interruption is the default state.
- A concrete environmental interaction problem in Wet Mode.
- A coherent product and voice system.
- Explicit PWA trade-offs.
- Transparent separation between measured interface properties and hypotheses.
- Clear disclosure of self-initiated status, research limitations, and AI assistance.

The principal issue is not lack of material. It is **signal dilution**. The current case study asks a reviewer to process competitive framing, persona details, theory, voice ratios, citations, multiple disclosures, implementation detail, and future measurement plans before the strongest decisions fully settle.

The portfolio version should make three arguments:

1. Edgar reframed disruption as a product requirement rather than user failure.
2. Edgar translated real environmental constraints into distinctive interaction decisions.
3. Edgar understands the difference between a designed property, a tested finding, and an untested hypothesis.

Everything that does not strengthen one of those arguments should be shortened, moved into an appendix, or removed.

## 2. Evidence boundary

### Verified from the supplied case study and repository

- Self-initiated concept project.
- Approximately ten weeks of solo work.
- Offline-first, bilingual ES/EN PWA framing.
- Strategy, IA, brand/voice system, design tokens, flows, interface work, and implementation are claimed as owned work.
- Wet Mode, Session Swapper, “Why” affordance, and bilingual onboarding are documented product concepts.
- Contrast ratios and interface dimensions are documented as measurements against design tokens.
- The repository contains HTML, CSS, JavaScript, case-study, deck, figure, and screenshot assets.

### Not verified as product outcomes

- Increased long-term adherence.
- Faster real-world poolside interaction.
- Reduced error rate.
- Retention, adoption, or commercial performance.
- Usability improvements with recruited participants.

### Required language rule

Never present the proposed ≥15% adherence improvement or ≥40% task-speed improvement as achieved outcomes. They are hypotheses and must remain labeled as such.

Preferred labels:

- **Measured design property**
- **Prototype observation**
- **Hypothesis**
- **Proposed success threshold**
- **Planned validation**
- **Unknown**

Avoid:

- “Result” when no user study or launch data exists.
- “Proven.”
- “Improved adherence.”
- “Users completed the task faster.”
- “Market gap” stated as an established industry fact.

## 3. Priority changes

### P0 — Required before portfolio publication

#### 3.1 Keep concept status visible

Place **Self-initiated concept — functional prototype, not launched to users** beside the project title. Do not rely on a disclosure near the bottom of the page.

#### 3.2 Separate measured properties from hypotheses

Create two visually distinct groups:

**Measured in the design/prototype**

- Token contrast ratios.
- Wet Mode target dimensions.
- Offline behavior implemented in the prototype.
- ES/EN interface coverage.

**Not yet validated with users**

- Adherence improvement.
- Wet-hand task-speed improvement.
- Error-rate improvement.
- Retention or engagement effects.

#### 3.3 Reframe the competitive claim

Current language implies that an entire market quadrant is empty. A limited product teardown cannot establish that conclusively.

Replace with:

> My teardown suggested an opportunity between analytics-heavy coaching products and socially driven fitness platforms: scientific depth delivered without pressure-based adherence mechanics.

#### 3.4 Add a direct product demonstration

The supplied links verify the case study and repository, but not a separate public product experience.

Add one of:

- A public prototype link.
- A 15–30-second captioned walkthrough.
- An embedded interaction recording with a static poster.
- A “Prototype unavailable publicly” note explaining what can be shown during an interview.

Do not label the case-study page itself as the live product.

#### 3.5 Make research limitations visible before the persona

Add this line immediately above any persona content:

> The Optimizer is a strategic synthesis based on secondary literature, product reviews, forum patterns, and informal conversations. It is not a validated persona from a recruited research panel.

### P1 — High-value restructuring

#### 3.6 Reduce the primary study to a five-minute read

Target:

- 1,200–1,800 words.
- 7–9 primary sections.
- 3 major decisions.
- 8–12 essential visuals.
- One optional appendix/deep-dive link.

#### 3.7 Move decisions before process and persona detail

Replace the current process-forward sequence with an outcome-and-decision sequence. A recruiter should reach Session Swapper and Wet Mode within the first two scrolls after the summary.

#### 3.8 Make “The meeting ran late” the narrative spine

Use that scenario throughout:

- What fails in the existing model?
- What does the product decide?
- What gets recalculated?
- What information stays hidden?
- What trade-off is accepted?
- How would the decision be tested?

#### 3.9 Show rejected alternatives visually

The current text says alternative IAs and Wet Mode prototypes existed, but the strongest versions are not visible.

Add:

- Calendar-first IA.
- Metrics-first IA.
- Program-first IA.
- Precision-tap Wet Mode.
- Earlier Spanish copy that was too literal.

For each, include one sentence explaining why it was rejected.

### P2 — Craft and polish

#### 3.10 Reduce voice-ratio detail

Keep the Sage/Hero framework as evidence of content-system thinking, but summarize it in one compact visual. Exact ratios across every surface are unnecessary unless targeting content-design or brand-system roles.

#### 3.11 Shorten theory and citations

Keep representative citations behind the “Why” interaction. Move the full citation methodology and effect-size rationale into an appendix.

#### 3.12 Add collaboration context honestly

Because this was solo work, do not invent a team. Instead explain:

- What decisions normally require product, engineering, research, or coaching input.
- What was simulated or self-reviewed.
- What would require subject-matter review before launch.

## 4. Recommended portfolio structure

### Section 1 — Hero

**Goal:** Establish project type, value, authorship, and status in one screen.

**Recommended title**

> Designing a coaching PWA that adapts when life interrupts training

**Recommended summary**

> Atlan is a self-initiated, offline-first coaching concept for bilingual endurance athletes. I designed and built the product around one premise: when a demanding week disrupts training, the system should adapt the plan instead of framing the athlete as a failure.

**Metadata**

- Role: Product strategy, UX/UI, brand system, and prototype implementation.
- Team: Solo concept.
- Duration: Approximately ten weeks.
- Platform: Offline-first PWA.
- Languages: Spanish and English.
- Status: Functional prototype; not launched to users.

**Hero proof chips**

- Offline-first
- ES/EN parity
- Wet Mode
- WCAG-aware interface system

Do not place hypothetical adherence or speed values in the hero.

### Section 2 — The 60-second case

Use four compact blocks:

**Situation**

> Endurance products often expose either dense performance analysis or social motivation. The target scenario required scientific depth without cognitive overload or punishment-based adherence.

**Responsibility**

> I owned strategy, product framing, IA, content and voice rules, design-system decisions, key flows, interface design, and prototype implementation.

**Intervention**

> I designed Session Swapper, Wet Mode, depth-on-demand explanations, and bilingual onboarding around environmental and cognitive constraints.

**Evidence status**

> The prototype and accessibility properties are documented. Product-behavior effects remain hypotheses pending recruited research and in-context testing.

### Section 3 — The decision that shaped the product

**Heading**

> The meeting ran late. The plan is now wrong.

Build a short decision narrative:

1. Tomorrow’s scheduled threshold session no longer fits.
2. A rigid product marks the session missed or asks the athlete to replan manually.
3. Atlan proposes a lower-cost alternative.
4. One gesture accepts or skips the change.
5. The week recalculates without exposing unnecessary complexity.

**Trade-off**

> Silent recalculation reduces control and transparency on the primary surface. Atlan compensates with an optional explanation layer rather than displaying periodization detail by default.

### Section 4 — Three product decisions

#### Decision 1: Adapt the week, not the blame

- Evidence: disruption scenario and adherence literature.
- Decision: Session Swapper.
- Rejected: red missed-state, manual calendar repair, or forced catch-up.
- Trade-off: less immediate control over the recalculated plan.
- Validation needed: comprehension, trust, acceptance rate, and plan-correction behavior.

#### Decision 2: Design for wet hands and sun glare

- Evidence: poolside context and offline requirement.
- Decision: gross-motor swipe zones, high contrast, minimal controls.
- Rejected: small precision buttons and connected-only behavior.
- Trade-off: reduced control density.
- Validation needed: real pool deck, waterproof pouch, wet hands, glare, false-positive gestures.

#### Decision 3: Keep science one tap below the surface

- Evidence: cognitive-load framing.
- Decision: “Why” affordance with source-backed explanations.
- Rejected: persistent charts and unexplained simplification.
- Trade-off: expert users take an additional action to inspect detail.
- Validation needed: discoverability, comprehension, and trust.

### Section 5 — System and interaction detail

Show:

- Session Swapper state flow.
- Wet Mode gesture grammar.
- Offline, cached, and reconnecting states.
- Empty, loading, error, pause, and completion states.
- ES/EN examples with culturally adapted copy.
- Contrast and target-size annotations.
- Reduced-motion behavior.

Avoid:

- Repeating full device mockups without annotation.
- Tiny UI inside oversized device frames.
- Decorative diagrams that do not support a decision.

### Section 6 — What is measured and what is not

Use a two-column evidence panel.

| Evidence available | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|
| Design-token contrast values | Moderated usability testing |
| Documented Wet Mode dimensions | In-context pool testing |
| Implemented offline behavior | Longitudinal adherence data |
| ES/EN prototype coverage | Native-speaker content validation |
| Documented measurement plans | Retention and adoption data |

Rename the existing “Outcomes” section to:

> Evidence and validation plan

### Section 7 — Reflection

Recommended copy:

> The concept became stronger when I stopped treating missed training as an edge case and made disruption the organizing condition. The largest limitation is equally clear: the audience and behavior assumptions have not been validated with a recruited panel. I would next test Wet Mode in a real pool environment, validate the disruption model with endurance athletes, and review the training logic and citations with a qualified coach or sports-science specialist.

### Section 8 — Credits and disclosure

Keep:

- Solo concept status.
- Research limitations.
- AI-assistance disclosure.
- Tools used for implementation acceleration.

Shorten to approximately 100–150 words.

### Optional appendix

Move the following out of the canonical portfolio path:

- Full competitive matrix.
- Detailed persona demographics.
- Complete adherence-science rationale.
- Full citation methodology.
- Exact voice ratios for every surface.
- Full validation protocol and statistical plan.
- Additional IA explorations.

## 5. Content to retain, revise, and remove

### Retain substantially as written

- “An emotionally intelligent coach for athletes with lives.”
- “Life-interruption is the default state, not the exception.”
- “The product’s job is to absorb disruption silently—not mark it red.”
- “Subtraction with redirection.”
- Wet Mode’s poolside rationale.
- PWA architectural trade-offs.
- Honest research and AI disclosures.
- Separation of measured and hypothetical claims.

### Revise

- “The upper-right quadrant is empty” → limited-teardown opportunity statement.
- Persona demographics → explicitly labeled design assumptions.
- Hypothesis tiles → research questions and success thresholds.
- “Outcomes” → evidence and validation plan.
- Long voice-ratio explanation → one system summary.
- Full research narrative → only the evidence that changed a decision.

### Remove from the main portfolio version

- Hypothetical values presented with outcome-like visual prominence.
- Broad claims about the entire endurance-tech market.
- Process-stage navigation as the principal story.
- Repeated disclosures.
- Exhaustive citations that interrupt the decision narrative.
- Any implied client, team, launch, or user-adoption claim.

## 7. Accessibility and performance requirements

### Accessibility

- Maintain WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline.
- Describe contrast values as design-token measurements, not proof of full-product compliance.
- Ensure visible keyboard focus on case-study navigation and interactive figures.
- Provide alt text that explains why each artifact matters.
- Give iframe demonstrations meaningful titles.
- Provide transcripts or step summaries for videos.
- Ensure figure captions remain selectable text.
- Preserve reading order when the side navigation collapses.
- Test at 200% and 400% zoom.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Performance

- Use responsive AVIF/WebP image sources.
- Avoid loading all embedded prototypes at initial page load.
- Use poster images and load demonstrations on intent.
- Reserve media dimensions to prevent layout shift.
- Preload only the actual hero asset.
- Lazy-load appendix figures.
- Confirm mobile interaction on a midrange phone and throttled connection.

## 11. Definition of done

The Atlan portfolio study is ready when a reviewer can answer these questions in under sixty seconds:

- What problem was being solved?
- What did Edgar personally own?
- What was the central design reframe?
- Which three decisions demonstrate judgment?
- What is actually measured?
- What remains hypothetical?
- What would be validated next?

The final impression should be:

> A product designer who can turn environmental and behavioral constraints into a coherent system—and who is disciplined about the limits of the evidence.
